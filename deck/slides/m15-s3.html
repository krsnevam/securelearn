MODULE 15

Governance, Compliance & Enterprise Risk

9 of 10

Concept tested: shadow AI governance. Background: OpenClaw, earlier called Clawdbot and then Moltbot, was renamed in late January 2026. Security researchers found hundreds of malicious skills in its skill marketplace distributing password-stealing malware, and the project's own maintainers warned it was unsafe for non-technical users. Model answer. 1) Most strong tables choose: remove immediately from high-risk roles such as finance leadership and anyone with privileged access; give everyone else a 30-day path to an approved, sandboxed alternative; do not simply ban, because the hour saved is real demand and bans drive tools underground. 2) Only approved AI agents may connect to company email, files or chat; personal agents must not hold company credentials; installing add-ons and skills requires approval. 3) Endpoint software inventory alerts, network monitoring for known agent traffic, and an amnesty channel so employees can request tools openly. Leadership point: shadow agents are the 2026 version of shadow IT, but with your credentials attached.
