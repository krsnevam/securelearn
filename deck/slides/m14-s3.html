1,100 purchase orders in 40 minutes

The situation

A supplier changes the format of its order confirmations. Your procurement agent reads them as failures and keeps re-ordering. No attacker is involved.

The twist: In 40 minutes it raises 1,100 duplicate purchase orders worth ₹6.8 crore. Twelve suppliers have already started dispatch.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Which detection should have fired, and when?
  2. Which level of stop, and who pulls it?
  3. How do we unwind the orders with suppliers?

SOC Integration & Runtime Defense

8 of 10

Concept tested: the runaway agent detection and graduated kill switches. Not every AI incident is an attack; cascading failure (OWASP ASI08) is just as costly. Model answer. 1) A rate rule: more than, say, five orders per supplier per hour, or any duplicate order for the same item within a day, should alert and pause within minutes. Ideally it fires after the first 10 orders, not 1,100. 2) Level 3: stop this agent and revoke its ordering access; the procurement head or the system owner can pull it, with the SOC executing. 3) Contact the twelve suppliers immediately with a cancellation list generated from the logs; check contract terms on cancellation charges. Leadership point: speed multiplies small errors. A rate limit is the cheapest control in agentic AI.
