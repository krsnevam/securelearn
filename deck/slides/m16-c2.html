Module 16 · Capstone case B

Realistic scenario · Finance

The vendor who wasn’t

The situation

Your accounts-payable agent processes an invoice email asking to change a key supplier’s bank account. A “supplier CFO” calls in a familiar voice to confirm. ₹3.2 crore is queued for the 4 p.m. payment run; treasury notices at 3:20.

Roles: CFO, Treasury Head, CISO, Procurement Head, General Counsel.

Your call · 30 min

  1. Release, hold, or split the payment run?
  2. Which control failed: agent, process or people?
  3. What must change before the next run?

MODULE 16

Capstone · Architecture, SOC & Governance Review

10 of 10

Capstone case B integrates agent impersonation and deepfakes (Module 11), trust architecture (Module 10) and production readiness (Module 13). Inject at minute 10: the real supplier cannot be reached because it is a public holiday in their state. Inject at minute 20: a second supplier email arrives with the same new bank details. Strong answers: hold only the affected payment and release the rest, so other suppliers are not harmed; verify through a contact already on file, never one from the email or call; the failure is in process design, because the agent was allowed to act on bank-change requests at all. Before the next run: bank changes removed from the agent's tools, call-back to numbers on file, dual approval, and a cooling-off period for first payments to new accounts. Also report to the bank and file a cyber-crime complaint if it is confirmed as fraud.
