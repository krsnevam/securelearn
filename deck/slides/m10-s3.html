Research finding · Mar 2026

The double agent in the cloud

What happened

Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 showed that an agent deployed on Google’s Vertex AI inherited broad default permissions from a platform service account. The researchers used them to extract credentials and reach restricted resources. Google revised its guidance.

Your situation: To hit a quarter-end launch, your team is deploying ten agents on a major cloud platform with default settings.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Who reviews the permissions an agent gets by default?
  2. What is the blast radius if one agent is hijacked?
  3. Delay the launch to fix identities, or not?

Source: Unit 42 research, March–April 2026, summarised in the OWASP GenAI Exploit Round-up, Q1 2026

MODULE 10

Enterprise Trust Architecture

4 of 10

Concept tested: least privilege and identity for agents. Model answer. 1) Cloud security, as part of the design gate; "default" is a decision someone made for you. 2) With inherited broad permissions, potentially every resource that service account can reach, which may extend beyond the project. 3) A short delay is usually right: give each agent its own identity with only the permissions it needs. If the business insists on the date, launch the lowest-risk agents first with restricted identities and hold the rest. Leadership point: cloud providers share responsibility, but configuration is yours. This links to the "assume breach" principle of Zero Trust.
