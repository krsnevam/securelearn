Realistic scenario · Fintech

The Friday-night prompt edit

The situation

On the Friday before Diwali, a product manager edits the support bot’s system prompt to promote a festive cashback offer. No test, no review: “it’s only text”.

The twist: By Sunday the bot has promised cashback to 9,000 customers on cards that do not qualify. The complaints are trending on social media.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Honour the promises, honour some, or refuse?
  2. Which gate should have stopped this edit?
  3. How does our change policy define a prompt change?

Concept tested: release and change management for prompts. Model answer. 1) Most tables conclude: honour the promises already made (the Air Canada ruling suggests you own what your AI says, and the reputational cost of refusing during a festival is high), stop the bot making new ones immediately, and contact affected customers proactively. Cost it out: 9,000 customers at the offer value. 2) A pre-release gate: an evaluation set that tests offers against eligibility rules, and an approval from product and compliance for any customer-facing commitment. 3) Any change to prompts, models, tools or knowledge sources that alters customer-facing behaviour is a production change: versioned, tested, approved and reversible. Leadership point: festive peaks are exactly when rushed changes happen. Set a change freeze, or a fast-track process with tests.
