Real attack trend · since 2024

LLMjacking: someone else’s AI bill

What happened

Sysdig named the attack “LLMjacking”: criminals steal cloud or API keys and run expensive AI models on the victim’s account, often reselling the access. Sysdig estimated the cost could exceed US$46,000 a day per victim.

Your situation: At 9 a.m., finance flags that one AI key used 30 times its normal volume overnight. It belongs to a pilot that ended last month.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Which detection should have fired first?
  2. Contain now: what exactly do we revoke?
  3. Who owns AI spend anomalies: finance, IT or the SOC?

Source: Sysdig Threat Research Team, LLMjacking reports, 2024

MODULE 14

SOC Integration & Runtime Defense

8 of 10

Concept tested: usage telemetry as a security signal, and the retirement step of model governance. Model answer. 1) A usage anomaly alert per key (volume, cost, time of day, new regions or models), feeding the SOC rather than only the monthly finance report. And an inventory check: the key should have been revoked when the pilot ended. 2) Revoke that key, rotate any keys stored in the same place, check where it leaked from (code repositories, CI logs, laptops), and set spending caps with the provider. 3) Joint ownership: the SOC owns the alert, finance owns the budget threshold, and every key has a named business owner. Leadership point: a forgotten pilot is a live liability; retirement is part of governance.
