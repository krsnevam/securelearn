Traps for agents on the open web

What happened

Forcepoint and Unit 42 found live web pages hiding instructions for AI agents: a $5,000 PayPal transfer, file-deletion commands, and a push to make an AI hiring screener approve fake candidates. Google measured a 32% rise in such content in three months.

Your situation: Your research and procurement agents browse supplier and competitor websites every day.

Your call · 15 min

  1. What may an agent do right after reading a web page?
  2. Which of our agents could move money or data?
  3. How would we know if one had been triggered?

MODULE 11

Attack Surface & Risk Landscape

5 of 10

Concept tested: indirect prompt injection is no longer theoretical; attackers are seeding the web for agents. The Google figure is a 32% relative increase in malicious injection content on crawled pages between November 2025 and February 2026. Model answer. 1) After reading untrusted content, an agent should be limited to read-only actions and drafting; payments, sending data out and running commands need fresh human approval. 2) Map them now: any agent with web access plus a payment, email or shell tool is the risky combination. 3) Log every tool call after browsing and alert on sensitive tool calls that follow external content. Live demo idea for the room: show a harmless hidden-text document that makes an assistant end its summary with a codeword.
