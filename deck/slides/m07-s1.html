Real case · May 2025

An AI hiring screener goes to court

What happened

In Mobley v. Workday, a US court let an age-discrimination claim over AI applicant screening proceed as a nationwide collective action. The court had already accepted that an AI vendor can be treated as the employer’s agent.

Your situation: HR wants a vendor’s AI to shortlist 40,000 campus applicants this season. The vendor says the model is “bias-tested” but will not share the results.

Your call · 15 min

  1. If the shortlist is biased, who is accountable: the vendor or us?
  2. What bias evidence do we demand before signing?
  3. At which step must a human make the decision?

Source: Mobley v. Workday, US District Court, Northern District of California; collective action certified May 2025

Responsible AI Frameworks

1 of 10

Principle tested: accountability, fairness, human oversight. Model answer. 1) Accountability cannot be outsourced: the deploying company answers to candidates and regulators, and this case shows the vendor may share liability too. 2) Demand selection rates by group (gender, age band, region, college tier), the adverse-impact ratio (the US four-fifths rule is a useful benchmark), testing on Indian applicant data, and contractual audit rights. "Bias-tested" without results is not evidence. 3) A human should review all rejections near the cut-off, or a random sample, and candidates should have a route to request review. Bonus point: hiring is listed as high-risk under the EU AI Act, so any EU candidates raise the stakes. Debrief question: would your procurement team have caught this?
