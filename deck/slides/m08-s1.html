Real case · Aug 2025

18,000 cups of water

What happened

Taco Bell’s drive-thru voice AI went viral after a customer ordered 18,000 cups of water, alongside clips of it looping on simple orders. The chain said it was rethinking where AI is used and when staff should take over, especially at busy times.

Your situation: Your voice agent takes orders and bookings for 600 outlets. Pranksters have started posting videos of tricking it.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Which signals should hand a call to a human instantly?
  2. Who can change those limits during a peak hour?
  3. How do we prove that handoffs actually work?

Source: public reporting (Wall Street Journal, BBC), August 2025

MODULE 08

Responsive AI & Runtime Adaptability

2 of 10

Concept tested: runtime policy and adaptive controls. Model answer. 1) Handoff triggers: order quantity or value outside normal range, repeated corrections, the customer asking for a person, low speech-recognition confidence, and abusive or nonsense input. 2) The operations duty manager, through a configuration screen, not a code release; changes are logged. 3) Track handoff rate, time to human, and mystery-shopper tests every week. Leadership point: the reputational cost here came from virality, not money. A one-line rule such as "quantities above 20 need staff confirmation" would have prevented the headline.
