Realistic scenario · Aviation

Rebooking two lakh passengers by morning

The situation

A week of mass cancellations hits a large Indian airline. Its rebooking agent can issue travel vouchers up to ₹10,000 without approval. Overnight, request volume jumps 40 times.

The twist: By 2 a.m. fraudsters are claiming vouchers for flights they never booked, while genuine passengers rage online about the waits.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Tighten autonomy, loosen it, or both by customer segment?
  2. Who can change the limits at 2 a.m.?
  3. What must the duty manager’s dashboard show?

2 of 10

Concept tested: adaptive risk controls; the same agent needs different limits for different risk signals. Model answer. 1) Both: loosen for verified passengers on cancelled flights (PNR matched to a cancelled flight, so instant voucher), tighten for unmatched or new accounts (human review, lower cap). 2) A named duty manager with pre-approved crisis settings, a "crisis mode" switch agreed before the crisis. 3) Voucher volume and value per hour, the match rate against cancelled PNRs, the fraud-flag rate, queue length and the handoff rate. Leadership point: crises are when autonomy is most valuable and most abused, so pre-agree the crisis playbook.
