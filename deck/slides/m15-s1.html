What happened

The EU’s 2026 Digital Omnibus moved high-risk AI Act duties, which cover uses such as hiring and credit, from August 2026 to December 2027. Prohibitions and general-purpose AI duties stayed in force.

Your situation: Your firm screens candidates with AI for two EU clients. The CFO proposes pausing the AI Act programme for a year to save ₹4 crore.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Is our hiring tool high-risk under the Act?
  2. Pause, slow down or continue, and why?
  3. What do we tell the EU clients?

Source: EU Digital Omnibus on AI, provisional agreement May 2026; Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

Model answer. 1) Yes: AI used for recruitment and candidate selection is listed in Annex III as high-risk, and the Act applies to non-EU providers whose system outputs are used in the EU. 2) Most strong tables choose "slow down, don't stop": keep the foundations that also serve ISO 42001, client contracts and India's DPDP duties (inventory, risk assessment, documentation, human oversight, logging), and re-phase the EU-specific conformity work to meet December 2027 with margin. EU clients will put these requirements in contracts long before the legal deadline. 3) Proactively share your compliance roadmap; it is a sales advantage. Leadership point: a delay is a planning window, not a reprieve. Use the ₹4 crore question to discuss how much of the programme is reusable across frameworks.
