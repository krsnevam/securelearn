Real case · Aug 2025

Bot in, people back

What happened

Commonwealth Bank of Australia cut 45 customer-service roles after launching an AI voice bot, citing falling call volumes. Volumes rose instead and managers were pulled onto phones. After a union challenge, the bank reversed the cuts and apologised.

Your situation: Your COO wants to cut 30% of contact-centre roles once the new voice agent goes live next quarter.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Which readiness evidence was missing at CBA?
  2. Which pilot metrics must we see first?
  3. What is our rollback plan if volumes rise?

Source: Finance Sector Union and Commonwealth Bank statements, public reporting, August 2025

MODULE 13

Secure Productization & Lifecycle

7 of 10

Concept tested: production readiness is a business question, not only a technical one. Model answer. 1) Evidence that the bot actually resolved calls end to end; volume forecasts that held up; customer satisfaction against the human baseline; and a fallback staffing plan. 2) Containment rate (calls fully resolved by the bot), repeat-call rate, transfer rate, customer satisfaction and complaint volume, measured for at least a full business cycle. 3) Keep capacity in reserve, retrain rather than release staff until targets are met, and agree in advance the thresholds that trigger rollback. Leadership point: AI readiness includes people and reputation. Workforce decisions tied to unproven AI are among the most visible failures.
