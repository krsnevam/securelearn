02:14 — the HR copilot sends a file

The situation

At 02:14 the SIEM flags that the HR copilot has emailed a file of 4,000 employee records to an external Gmail address. Seconds earlier, it summarised a résumé uploaded through the careers portal.

The twist: The HR head is on a flight. The copilot is running 300 other tasks overnight, including payroll checks due at 6 a.m.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Severity, and which kill-switch level?
  2. Who do we wake, and in what order?
  3. Which reporting clocks have just started?

SOC Integration & Runtime Defense

8 of 10

Concept tested: detection to containment. Pattern: indirect injection through an uploaded résumé, followed by data exfiltration through the email tool. Model answer. 1) Critical severity. Start at level 1 (disable the email tool) immediately, then level 2 (read-only) so payroll checks can still run in read mode; move to level 3 if any further anomaly appears. 2) SOC lead, then CISO, then the copilot's system owner, then the data protection officer and legal; HR is briefed when reachable. Ask the email provider to recall or quarantine the message. 3) CERT-In directions require reporting of specified cyber incidents within six hours of noticing them; DPDP breach intimation duties apply from May 2027 but follow current policy now; contractual notice to any clients whose data is involved. Debrief: what in the architecture should have blocked this? (Approval for external email with attachments; no outbound mail after reading uploads.)
