Research finding · Jan 2026

A meeting invite that briefs the attacker

What happened

Miggo Security sent a calendar invite with dormant instructions hidden inside. Later, when the user asked Gemini to summarise their day, it copied private meeting details into an event the attacker could see.

Your situation: Your CXOs want an executive-assistant agent with access to their mail, calendar and document drive, by next month.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Which inputs to this agent are untrusted?
  2. What should it never do right after reading them?
  3. Which data should be out of its reach entirely?

Source: Miggo Security research, January 2026, summarised by the Cloud Security Alliance, April 2026

MODULE 09

Architecture Review Workshop

3 of 10

Concept tested: reviewing an architecture for indirect injection. Model answer. 1) Anything an outsider can send: emails, calendar invites, shared documents, meeting titles and descriptions. Executives receive a lot of these. 2) Create or edit calendar events, send email, or share files, without the executive confirming. 3) Board papers, M&A folders and HR files should be outside its default scope, available only on explicit request. Leadership point: executive assistants are the highest-value target because CXO inboxes hold the most sensitive data and receive the most outside content. Start with read-only plus drafts that a human sends.
