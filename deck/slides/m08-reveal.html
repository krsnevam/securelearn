The human in the loop was the weakest link

The tempting answer · A

“It’s the AI.” Blaming the model feels natural, and a human approver feels safe.

Why it fails

A fixed limit invites splitting. The approver read the agent’s own summary, written from the attacker’s invoice.

What works

Cumulative limits per vendor and day; changed beneficiaries always escalate; approvers see source data, not AI prose.

Responsive AI & Runtime Adaptability

2 of 10

Key insight: human oversight is a control only if the human sees independent evidence and the threshold cannot be gamed. Splitting payments to stay under an approval limit is a classic fraud pattern (structuring); agents can do it at machine speed. Runtime policy should judge patterns (cumulative value, new beneficiaries, unusual timing), not single transactions. Group deliverable for this module: NEXUS v2 approval rules that cannot be split or faked.
