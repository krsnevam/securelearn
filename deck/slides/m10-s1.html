Module 10 · Scenario 1 of 3

Real case · Aug 2025

Stolen keys from a chat widget

What happened

Attackers stole the access tokens used by Salesloft’s Drift AI chat integration. With them, they pulled data from the Salesforce systems of hundreds of companies, including several major security vendors.

Your situation: Over the past year, teams have connected more than 30 AI tools to email, CRM and file storage. Nobody holds a full list of their tokens.

Your call · 15 min

  1. Who owns the inventory of AI integrations and their tokens?
  2. Could we revoke one vendor’s tokens within an hour?
  3. What scope and expiry rules apply from now on?

Source: Google Threat Intelligence Group advisory (UNC6395) and vendor disclosures, August–September 2025

MODULE 10

Enterprise Trust Architecture

4 of 10

Concept tested: supply chain trust and token misuse. The attacker never touched the victims' own systems directly; they used a trusted integration's keys. Model answer. 1) Security owns the inventory, but each integration needs a business owner who justifies it. Most organizations can find these tokens in their SaaS admin consoles (connected apps). 2) Test it: run a revocation drill for one vendor and time it. 3) Least scope (read only what is needed), short expiry with refresh, IP restrictions where possible, and alerts on unusual bulk data pulls by integrations. Leadership point: every "connect your CRM" button on an AI tool creates a long-lived key to your data held by someone else.
